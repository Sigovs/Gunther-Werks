1 · Replaces “House Lights”Programs: “Four programs. One 993.”

One section, read top to bottom in one order: choose (the four programs are the navigation) → see (one large car on the same registered base) → understand (three facts, the same three for every program) → act (one button). Special editions move to their own dark band below, so the two groups can never be confused.

Which program is active

  • Desktop: active name in full ink with a 2 px bar over its column; the other three at 42 % ink.
  • Phone: active segment filled in ink.
  • Tabs use role="tab" and aria-selected.

How to move

  • Mouse: click a name.
  • Keyboard: ←/→ moves between tabs, Home/End jump to the ends.
  • Touch: tap a segment, or swipe the car.
  • Scroll never changes the program.

What distinguishes it

  • The same three facts for every program, so the four compare: Power · Engine · Signature.
  • F-26: 1,000 hp · 4.0 L twin-turbo · slantnose, after the 930 Flachbau.
  • Turbo: 850 hp (track mode) · 4.0 L twin-turbo · hydraulic nose lift.
  • GWR: 500+ hp at 9,000 rpm · 4.0 L naturally aspirated · S-duct hood, ventilated ducktail.
  • GWX: 440 hp · 4.0 L second generation · Candy Purple, exclusive.
  • One source line under the program line.

Next action

  • “Enquire about the F-26”: one solid button, preselects the program in Contact.
  • Secondary: the program at guntherwerks.com ↗.

Motion

  • Program change: the new car dissolves in over the old one, which drops out late (from F).
  • The active bar slides between columns.
  • No pin, no scroll-driven light-up.
  • Reduced motion: instant swap.

Removed from F

  • The pinned dark-to-light scrub.
  • Leader-line callouts.
  • The four-line spec list and the tiny switcher.
  • Special editions on the same stage.

Sources

  • GW programme pages and releases.
  • F-26 1,000 hp per the 15 Aug 2025 release; GW's site also says 1,067.
  • GWX 440 hp per the 13 Aug 2025 GWX release; the site states no figure.

2 · Replaces the scroll-scrubbed filmGoodwood: “On the hill at Goodwood.”

Footage test first, as asked. The F-26 clip was played as a plain muted loop at normal speed, in two variants: _contracts/goodwood-loop-test/loop-A is the best 4.4 s take three times; loop-B is the full 21 s clip twice.

  • It is event coverage, with cuts at 2.5, 7, 9, 14.5, 17.5 and 19 s.
  • For most of the clip the car is small in a wide frame. There is a dark tree tunnel and a blown frame at 17 s.
  • The one strong take (head-on approach) is soft telephoto with heat shimmer. It cannot loop seamlessly: the car approaches, then jumps back.

Verdict: it does not hold attention, so the chapter becomes a still composition. GW's own start-line photograph carries it; the four-day log carries the information. No video, no scrub, no oversized figure.

Composition

  • One dominant photograph (F-26 at the START gantry, exhaust flames, marshal), with nothing laid over the car.
  • Beside it: dates, title, one line, the four-day log, and a link to GW's behind-the-scenes page.

Claims (all from GW's two Goodwood posts)

  • 9–12 July 2026.
  • F-26 and Turbo on the 1.16-mile hillclimb.
  • Thu: Turbo, Kaleb Ngatoa and Max Orido. Fri: F-26, Liam Lawson. Sat: F-26, Scott Speed, Shoot-Out qualifying. Sun: F-26, Scott Speed, Timed Shoot-Out.
  • No times or results are claimed, because GW publishes none.

Motion

  • None required.
  • Optional: a slow 4 % scale settle on the photo as it enters (transform only), disabled under reduced motion.

Removed from F

  • The 115-frame canvas scrub and its pin.
  • Captions keyed to the film.
  • “1,000 HP” set at display size.
  • The film pause control.

Effect on the page

  • One fewer pin.
  • About 1.1 screens shorter at 1920.
  • 5.4 MB of frames (1.6 MB on phones) no longer load.
