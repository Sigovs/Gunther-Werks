## 1. The idea

> **Gunther Werks is black and white and every colour on a car is somebody's, so the page runs a commission down the
> atelier's own line in monochrome and lets colour in at one station only — paint — after which the car is the only
> coloured thing in its world.**

The spine is Gunther Werks' own build sequence, the six "pillars" on its home page, in its own order and words:
**Customization · Donor Car Disassembly · Fabrication · Body Stage · Paint & Finishing · Assembly** (DICT
`home.pillar.*`). Every station has real GW photography behind it (§4), so the page claims nothing GW has not shown.

The colour rule is the whole art direction, and it is a fact before it is a style: GW's identity is strictly black and
white and its only colour comes from the cars (CI §3.3; brief); its commissions are tailored in "Color, trim, aero, and
mechanical character" so the car "carries both Gunther Werks' signature and the owner's own fingerprint" (DICT
`page.commissions.pillar_vision_*`). Black and white is the signature; colour is the fingerprint. On a real line,
colour arrives at the paint booth, fifth of six stations. So does it here.

**The rule, stated once for every builder:** colour lives on a car's own surfaces (paint, leather, trim) and nowhere
else. People, tools, rooms, roads, skies, UI and type are black and white. Before station 05 there is no colour on the
page except what the hero gives away and what the visitor asks for (§4/01). After 05, every car is in colour and its
world is not.

**Why this is Concept B and not a variant of A**

| | A — "same 993, remastered" | B — "The Line" |
|---|---|---|
| Governing question | *What* changed? (space, comparison) | *How* it becomes yours (time, process) |
| Grammar (DNA36) | Layered vertical page, cove → plate → film | Chaptered editorial journal, 00–10, with one horizontal build-line track |
| Register | Dark cove first, light plate second | White paper + black-and-white documentary photography, colour only on cars. No dark cove ground anywhere |
| Hero | F-26 profile, warm cove, static | Orange Turbo driving away at sunset, full colour, then the colour drains under scroll |
| Signature | House Lights (cove → studio, pull back to row) | First Coat (colour enters the page at the paint station, one scrubbed pass) |
| Lineup | Four left-facing profiles, one scale, one floor; "pull it out" hover | Four front-3/4 cars in grey at station 01; the one you point at takes its colour |
| Film | Goodwood F-26 drive-past, the peak | End Game crane-down reveal at delivery; no Goodwood film |
| Ending | Amber Turbo cove + form | "00": the conversation that starts a build, over GW's sketch and clay |
| Scroll architecture | 2 vertical pins | 1 horizontal pinned track with a pin-stop inside it, 4 vertical pins, 4 slide-over overlaps |

**Territories considered (the three the brief offered):**
- *(1) Persistent subject* — **killed for B.** One car held while the world changes is A's sentence ("hold the
  silhouette still … let the context change") at page scale. House Lights already performs it. It would be A in a
  second costume (CG6).
- *(2) Selective-colour journal on its own* — **not enough on its own.** It opens on a treatment (CG1), and selective
  colour without a reason is a 2005 photo-filter cliché. It is kept as B's look because (3) gives it a meaning: colour
  = paint = ownership.
- *(3) The Build* — **chosen as the spine**, because GW's own material supports every step honestly: a stripped 993 on a
  lift, CNC, layup, a carbon body on a dolly, a freshly painted shell on a dolly, the engine, the trim shop, the key box.
  B = (3) carrying (2).

**Honesty constraint, binding on copy:** the station photographs show *different* cars and parts. The page follows *a
commission down the line*, never "this car". No caption says or implies that one car is pictured throughout. The orange
of the hero and the orange of the painted shell are a chromatic rhyme, not a claim of identity, and the shell's model is
not named (GALLERY tags it "Turbo [I]", inferred).

**Concept gate (advice, run anyway):** CG1 opens on a brand fact (B/W identity, owner's colour), not a style · CG2 one
sentence, and it could be wrong: if collectors don't care how the car is made, a build story delays the sale · CG3 make
the page full-colour throughout and the paint station is just another photo; make the line vertical and the conveyor
reading weakens: the techniques carry meaning · CG4 static build (§7 reduced motion) still reads: grey stations, a
before/after shell, colour after it · CG5 no shared moves with A or the sibling sites (table above) · CG6 one
direction · CG7 the colour rule runs to the footer (§4 "Carries the idea" lines).

## 4. Shot list — every section, in order

### Persistent chrome — the header is the build sheet

- **Always visible, fixed, 64 px (56 px phone). Never scrolls away, never turns into a solid bar.**

  **(305) 783-6493** (tel link) · **Enquire** (1 px stroke pill, GW's CTA form, CI §3.6).

- **Tone follows the ground:** each section declares `data-ground="light|dark|photo"`; type flips black ↔ white at

## 5. The signature move — **First Coat**

**What it is.** The page is grey from the moment the hero drains. At station 05 the horizontal line stops in front of a
freshly painted body shell shown in black and white, and the scroll *applies* its colour: one pass travels from roof to
sill, the way a spray pass does, and the shell turns orange behind it. In the glossy floor the reflection fills the
opposite way — from the bottom of the reflection up toward the sill — because that is what a mirror does. Then a single
highlight crosses the bonnet (finishing), and the line moves on. From that frame onward, cars on this page are in colour
and nothing else is.

**Why it belongs to Gunther Werks.** GW's own build sequence puts Paint & Finishing fifth of six (DICT); GW's identity
is black and white with colour only from the cars (CI §3.3); and GW photographed exactly this moment — a painted shell,
head-on, on a dolly, in its own bay (`craft-27`, a GW home-page image). A brand without its own paint stage, or without a
monochrome identity, has nothing for this move to mean.

**Mechanics (desktop ≥ 1024), inside the line's pin:**
- The track timeline has three segments: travel to 05 → **hold 140vh** → travel to 06. The hold is part of the same
  scrubbed timeline (`scrub: 0.6`), so reversing scroll un-paints the shell.
- Layers, bottom to top: SHELL-G (the square, graded B/W) · REFL-C (reflection, colour, alpha) · SHELL-C (shell, colour,
  alpha, no reflection) · a highlight layer clipped to the shell's alpha · the inset · HTML type.
- **The pass:** SHELL-C and REFL-C are revealed through a soft-edged band (feather ≈ 10 % of the shell's height).
  Implement as one WebGL quad (three textures + a progress uniform) or a 2D canvas composite; animate **no** CSS
  `mask-position` on a 2560 px image without a profile proving 60 fps. Fallback: transform-only reveal (wrapper
  `translateY(-100%→0)` with the child counter-translated) with a pre-feathered edge baked into the alpha.
- Hold timeline (0–100 % of 140vh):
  - 0–8 %: track has stopped; numeral **05** settles; eyebrow and H2 masked-rise.
  - 8–58 %: the pass, roof (y ≈ 32 % of the image) → sill (≈ 66 %), linear. REFL-C runs the mirrored pass with an 8 %
    lag. The square scales 1.00 → 1.06 about its centre — the camera leaning in (the moodboard push-in).
  - 58–80 %: finishing — one soft specular streak (10 % width, white at 35 %, `screen`) crosses the bonnet left → right,
    clipped to the shell. **Cut it if at 1× it reads as a UI shimmer rather than light on paint.** The finishing inset
    (`craft-26`, 24vw) rises over the square's lower-right corner at 1.25×.
  - 80–100 %: the caption line fades up; the header readout is already **05 — PAINT & FINISHING**.
- **Phone (≤ 1023, authored):** the line is vertical (§6); 05 pins for 100svh with the 390 × 390 square; the same pass,
  the same mirrored reflection, push 1.00 → 1.04, no inset (it stacks below, static).
- **Reduced motion:** the split still (§4/05). **No-JS:** the same still.
- **Keyboard / Page Down:** a Page Down lands mid-pass and shows a designed frame (half-painted shell is a legible
  state); the hold is long enough that two Page Downs cross it.
